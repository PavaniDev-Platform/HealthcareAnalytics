

















# Emergency Room (ER) Analytics & Reporting Specification

---

## 1. Executive ER Overview

### Key Departmental Metrics

- **Total Admissions:** Total number of patients / emergency admissions per department.
- **Average Wait Time:** Average wait time of patients in each department.
  - How long before some one evaluates the patient?
  - How quickly is acuity determined?
  - Are patients sitting unassessed?
- **Patient Satisfaction:** Satisfaction score of each department.
- **30-Day Readmission Rate:** Readmission rate within 30 days for each department.
- **Revenue Generation:** How much revenue is generated by each department.

### Insurance Mix

- **Admission Trends:** Admission trends based on insurance type.
- **Triage Wait Time:** Triage wait time based on insurance type.
- **Satisfaction Score:** Satisfaction score based on insurance type *(do patients with certain insurance plans have better satisfaction rates than others?)*
- **30-Day Readmission Rate:** 30-day readmission rate based on insurance type.
- **Billing:** Total amount billed to each insurance provider.

---

## 2. Operational ER Overview

### Departmental Workload & Staffing

- **Busiest Departments:** Departments attending the highest number of critical emergencies (`triage_level = critical`). *(See* *[Triage Level (ESI)](#5-triage-level-esi)* *for definitions.)*
- **Staffing Balance (Understaffed vs. Overstaffed):** Comparison of number of physicians vs. emergency visits in each department.
- **Average Length of Stay (LOS):** Average length of stay of patients in each emergency department.

### Critical vs. Non-Critical Mix

- **Busiest Departments:** Number of critical vs. non-critical emergencies per department.

- **Staffing Balance by Acuity:**

  - Number of physicians working on **ESI = 1 or 2** (critical).
  - Number of physicians working on **non-critical emergencies** (`ESI > 2`).
  - *Analytical Note:* Physicians working on ESI 1 and 2 may attend fewer patients per shift than physicians working on ESI > 2 due to higher acuity and care complexity.

- **Length of Stay by Acuity:** Length of stay (LOS) for critical vs. non-critical emergencies in the ER.

---

## 3. Admission Trends

### Temporal & Demographic Patterns

- **Peak Months:** Which months have experienced the highest number of emergencies? *(Monthly granularity only; daily trends are excluded.)*
- **Primary Emergency Causes by Age:** Main emergency cause broken down by age group.

### Critical vs. Non-Critical Mix (Age & Department Breakdown)

- **Admissions Volume:** Age-group-based critical and non-critical admissions in each department.
- **Satisfaction Scores:** Age-group-based satisfaction for critical and non-critical admissions in each department.
- **30-Day Readmission Rate:** Age-group-based 30-day readmission rate for critical and non-critical admissions in each department.

---

## 4. Quality of Care

- **By Diagnosis:** Which diagnoses have the highest readmission rates?
- **By Age Group:** Which age groups have the highest readmission rates?
- **By Insurance Group:** Which insurance groups have the highest readmission rates?

---

## 5. Triage Level (ESI)

Most hospitals use the **Emergency Severity Index (ESI)** on a scale from **1 to 5**, where **1** is the most critical:

| ESI Level | Classification | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Most Critical** | Immediate life-saving intervention required. |
| **2** | **Emergent** | High-risk situation where the patient is stable for the moment, but the condition could quickly become life-threatening. |
| **3** | **Urgent** | Stable, but the patient requires multiple hospital resources (such as lab work, X-rays, or IV medications). |
| **4** | **Less Urgent** | Stable and requires only one hospital resource. |
| **5** | **Non-Urgent** | Stable and requires no hospital resources other than a general physician examination and prescription. |
